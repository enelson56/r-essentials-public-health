EM or Confounding by Antimicrobial Use? Example Write-Up
The results suggest that antimicrobial use is not an important effect modifier of the relationship between previous hospitalization and MRSA infection.

Among individuals who used antimicrobials, those with a previous hospitalization had approximately 1.18 times the risk of MRSA infection compared with those without a previous hospitalization. Similarly, among individuals who did not use antimicrobials, those with a previous hospitalization had approximately 1.34 times the risk of MRSA infection compared with those without a previous hospitalization.

Because the stratum-specific risk ratios are relatively similar, there is little evidence of meaningful effect modification. We can then evaluate potential confounding by comparing the crude RR (approximately 1.36) with the Mantel–Haenszel adjusted RR (approximately 1.29). The relatively small difference suggests that antimicrobial use is not a substantial confounder of this association.
